A short, complete ask — and the hour on Friday when asking is answered

Sahih Muslim 2721 · Sahih al-Bukhari 935

Reference, virtue & method

Reference

“There is an hour on Friday and if a Muslim gets it while asking Allah for something, He will definitely meet his demand.”

The hadith about the hour does not name a dua to say. The dua here is a separate authentic ask (Muslim 2721). The two are placed together for practice, and the page does not claim the Prophet ﷺ specified this dua for that hour.

Sahih Sahih Muslim 2721 · the hour: Sahih al-Bukhari 935 Open source ↗

Why we read it

  • Four words in Arabic that cover the whole of what a person actually needs: guidance, piety, restraint and freedom from want.
  • It is short enough to say repeatedly, which is the point of the Friday hour.
  • It teaches the adab of asking: you ask Allah directly, in His own words, without inventing phrases.

How to recite it

  • Say it on Friday, and make your own dua in your own language alongside it — the hadith only says to ask.
  • The scholars' two strongest positions on the hour are: the last part of the day before Maghrib, and the time the imam sits for the khutbah. Both are opinions, not text.
  • Keep asking even if you do not feel an answer on the day; the narration is about Allah answering, not about what you feel.
