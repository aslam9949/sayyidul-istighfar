Reference, virtue & method

Reference

“Whoever says it a hundred times in a day will have the reward of freeing ten slaves, a hundred good deeds will be written for him, a hundred sins will be erased, and it will be a protection for him from Shaytan that day until evening.”

Narrated in al-Bukhari and Muslim; some narrations give ten times instead of a hundred.

Sahih Sahih al-Bukhari 3293, 6403 · Sahih Muslim 2691 Open source ↗

Why we read it

  • It is pure tawhid with the reason attached: the kingdom, the praise, and the power all belong to Him.
  • The narration attaches a concrete reward for repeating it on a single day — one of the few dhikr with that clarity.
  • It costs nothing and needs no place: it is the easiest daily baseline to keep.

How to recite it

  • Say it in the morning; count on your fingers or a tally app so the count is real.
  • Aim for a hundred a day as in the narration, or ten if you are starting — the narration allows both.
  • Say it with attention on lahul-mulku wa lahul-hamdu, not as a rushed sound.
