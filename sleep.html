Two lines you say every night and every morning

Reference, virtue & method

Reference

“When he intended to go to bed he would say: Bismika Allahumma amutu wa ahya. And when he woke: Alhamdulillahilladhi ahyana…”

Two separate adhkar in one narration: one for lying down and one for waking. They are taught on this page as two lines so each gets its own clip.

Sahih Sahih al-Bukhari 6324 Open source ↗

Why we read it

  • They are the first and last words of your day, which makes them the easiest habit to keep.
  • They frame sleep as something given and taken, not a switch you control.
  • Both are extremely short, so there is no excuse not to learn them.

How to recite it

  • Say the first when you lie down; if you forget, say it and then sleep — do not skip the night.
  • Say the second as soon as you wake, before you pick up the phone.
  • Learn the meaning of "amutu wa ahya" once; the line becomes far easier to keep when it is not just sounds.
