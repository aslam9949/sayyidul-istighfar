Reference, virtue & method

Reference

“Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min al-hammi wal-hazan, wal-'ajzi wal-kasal, wal-jubni wal-bukhl, wa dala'i ad-dayn, wa ghalabati ar-rijal.”

Narrated from Anas ibn Malik (RA); the Prophet ﷺ used to say it frequently.

Sahih Sahih al-Bukhari 6369 Open source ↗

Why we read it

  • It names eight burdens in one breath: worry, grief, incapacity, laziness, cowardice, stinginess, the burden of debt, and being overpowered by others.
  • It covers both the inner state (hamm, hazan) and the outer trap (dayn, ghalabat ar-rijal).
  • It is a refuge (a'udhu), which is the honest posture when the problem is bigger than your planning.

How to recite it

  • Say it in the morning and evening, and before sleeping.
  • When debt or anxiety presses, say it slowly and name the specific burden you are handing over.
  • Learn the eight words individually so the dua is not a blur.
