Allahumma hab li hamdan — grant me praise and honour

Reference, virtue & method

Reference

This wording is reported from the Companion Sa'd ibn Ubadah (RA), not from the Prophet ﷺ. It is kept here as a companion's dua and is labelled asathar so the source is never confused with a hadith.

Athar, not a Prophetic hadith Athar of Sa'd ibn Ubadah (RA), Makarim al-Akhlaq

Why we read it

  • It asks for praise and honour, then immediately sets their price: honour only through deeds, deeds only through halal means.
  • The last line is a hard honesty: a little is not enough for me, and I am not suited to a little.
  • It stays useful at work: it keeps dignity tied to action instead of claims.

How to recite it

  • Recite it with the meaning in front of you — the value is in the trade-off it names.
  • Use it before and after work or earning, not as a charm for results.
  • Because it is an athar, do not treat it as a fixed sunnah with a promised reward.
