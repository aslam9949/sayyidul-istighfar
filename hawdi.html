The short form: refuge from worry and grief

Reference, virtue & method

Reference

“Allahumma inni a'udhu bika min al-hammi wal-hazan — O Allah, I seek refuge in You from worry and grief.”

This is the opening phrase of the longer dua in Lesson 5. It is kept separately as the short version for repetition, and the lesson note says so plainly.

Sahih Sahih al-Bukhari 6369 Open source ↗

Why we read it

  • It is the shortest way to name the two most common inner burdens: hamm (worry about what is coming) and hazan (grief over what has passed).
  • Because it is one line, it can be repeated in a lift, a car, or a wait — which is how dhikr actually survives a day.
  • It is the same wording as the first line of the longer version, so learning this one half-learns the next.

How to recite it

  • Repeat it in sets of three when worry rises; the short form is for frequency, not for once.
  • Use the longer version (Lesson 5) when you have a quiet moment and this one when you do not.
  • Say it in the morning and evening as part of your adhkar.
